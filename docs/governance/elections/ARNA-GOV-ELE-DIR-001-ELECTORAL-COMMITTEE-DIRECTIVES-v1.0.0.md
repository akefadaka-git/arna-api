## Proposed Electoral Committee Directives Framework

- **Document ID:** ARNA-GOV-ELE-DIR-001
- **Version:** 1.0.0
- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Status:** Proposed for review and approval; **not adopted**
- **Related specification:** [ARNA-GOV-ELE-SPEC-001 — Board Election System](./ARNA-GOV-ELE-SPEC-001-BOARD-ELECTION-SYSTEM-v1.0.0.md)

> **Approval notice:** This is a proposed framework, not an adopted company directive, General Assembly resolution, certified legal opinion, or authority to conduct an election. It must be conformed to applicable law, the certified operative Memorandum/Articles and valid resolutions of the competent company organs. Unresolved powers and procedures must not be inferred.

## Directive 01 — Establishment, mandate and authority

1. The Electoral Committee may act only after appointment by the competent company organ under applicable law and the operative corporate instruments.
2. The appointment instrument must define the committee's mandate, scope, term, reporting line, resources and limits of authority.
3. The committee administers the election; it does not amend law, the Memorandum/Articles, Board composition or shareholder voting rights.
4. Composition, membership count, appointment method, quorum, chair, secretary, alternates, removal and dissolution require formal approval.

## Directive 02 — Committee procedure and decisions

1. Maintain a written work plan, meeting schedule, agenda, attendance and minutes.
2. Record the question, evidence, participants, conflicts, decision and reasons for each material determination.
3. Restrict sensitive election information by role and log access.
4. Urgent decisions must be documented; undocumented oral instruction cannot change election rules.

## Directive 03 — Independence, impartiality and ethics

1. Disclose actual, potential and perceived conflicts before appointment and throughout service.
2. A conflicted member must recuse under approved rules; recusal and replacement must be recorded.
3. Protect confidentiality, refuse improper influence and report suspected interference.
4. Never use privileged access to identify secret ballot choices or alter voter entitlements.

## Directive 04 — Election initiation and timetable

1. An election begins only after a valid authorizing resolution is recorded.
2. The approved plan must specify seats, term, record date, nomination and objection/appeal windows, voting dates/channels, notice method and responsible authorities.
3. Notices must meet the legally applicable notice period. The scanned bylaw's apparent 30-day period and the statutory notice rule require legal reconciliation.
4. Postponement or cancellation requires authority, reasons, notice and preservation of prior records.

## Directive 05 — Shareholder voter register and rights

1. The electorate consists of eligible shareholders in the authoritative register at the approved record date.
2. Voting weight follows applicable share/class rules and any valid voting cap; do not assume one person/one vote.
3. Reconcile, review, freeze and version the register before voting.
4. Provide a controlled correction/challenge process before register lock.
5. The founding shareholder list is not proof of current shareholder status or current voting entitlement.

## Directive 06 — Nominations and candidate eligibility

1. Accept nominations only during the approved period and through the authorized route.
2. Apply the current Commercial Code and operative company eligibility criteria.
3. Confirm any company-specific shareholding requirement against the certified operative instrument and current law before enforcement.
4. Rejections must be reasoned, evidenced and open to the approved objection/appeal process.

## Directive 07 — Campaign and candidate conduct

1. Provide equal access to approved channels and materially equivalent publication opportunities.
2. Campaigns must comply with law and approved company rules.
3. Prohibit coercion, bribery, threats, misuse of company resources, impersonation, misinformation and retaliation, subject to approved enforcement procedures.
4. Committee decisions must be impartial, documented and appealable as approved.

## Directive 08 — Proxy voting

1. Require valid written, dated and signed authorization or other legally approved evidence.
2. Record shareholder, proxy, scope, meeting/election, receipt time, validity and revocation/replacement.
3. Comply with the Commercial Code and operative instruments, including applicable deposit deadlines.
4. Enforce personal/proxy mutual exclusion where law requires.
5. Proxy exercise cannot exceed the shareholder's verified entitlement. Do not invent proxy rules in software.

## Directive 09 — Physical voting

1. Record polling stations, custodians, tellers and observers under approved procedure.
2. Verify identity and entitlement before ballot issue; do not link the marked ballot to voter identity.
3. Log ballot boxes, seals, custody, opening/closing and unused, spoiled or challenged ballots.
4. Accessible assistance must preserve free choice and ballot secrecy.

## Directive 10 — Electronic voting and security

1. Electronic voting remains disabled until separately authorized by the Owner and competent company organ, legally reviewed, threat-modelled, independently security-assessed and accepted through A4.
2. If approved, require strong authentication, eligibility enforcement, single-use entitlement, ballot secrecy, tamper evidence and independent verification.
3. Segregate identity/eligibility from ballot selection; no ordinary administrator may reconstruct identity-choice links.
4. Approve key custody, incident response, recovery, availability, accessibility and oversight before activation.
5. Electronic meeting participation or vote-counting provisions must not be treated as automatic authorization for a remote secret-ballot system.

## Directive 11 — Counting, recounting and reconciliation

1. Authorized tellers count under a documented procedure; observers participate as approved.
2. Retain per-candidate/seat tally sheets and signatures as required.
3. Reconcile register, check-in, issued, cast, spoiled, invalid, challenged and unused ballots and voting weight.
4. A recount creates a linked new record; preserve the original count and reason for recount.
5. A material unresolved discrepancy blocks certification.

## Directive 12 — Complaints and appeals

1. Approve complaint categories, standing, channels, deadlines, decision-maker, evidence standards and remedies before the election.
2. Separate intake, investigation, decision and appeal roles to the extent required by the approved governance model.
3. Preserve evidence and chain of custody.
4. Pause certification when an unresolved matter legally affects the result.

## Directive 13 — Evidence, records and archive

1. Maintain the authorizing resolution, notices, register snapshots, nomination files, proxy records, station logs, custody evidence, tally sheets, reconciliation reports, complaints, minutes and certification documents.
2. Records must be attributable, time-stamped, tamper-evident and retained under approved company/legal rules.
3. Log access, export, correction, redaction and transfer.
4. Deliver an indexed archive manifest to the authorized custodian.

## Directive 14 — Result approval, certification and handover

1. Distinguish provisional results from final certified results.
2. Certify only after reconciliation, complaint/appeal and approval checks pass.
3. Only the legally competent authority may certify.
4. The certificate records the election, seats, candidates, votes/weight, outcome, exceptions, approvals, date and evidence references.
5. Handover the final report and archive; committee closure/dissolution follows its approved appointment instrument.

## Legal reference notes

The A1 legal scan considered the Ethiopian Commercial Code Proclamation No. 1243/2021, including Articles 296–300 (Board composition, eligibility, appointment and term), Article 331 (Supervisory Board), Articles 362 and 370 (General Meeting and notice), Articles 373 and 377–378 (proxy and attendance), Article 380 (tellers/counting), Articles 383–385 (voting weight, caps and conflicts), and Article 387 (minutes).
