## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes a consistent governance framework for ARNA committees and the procurement, sales, control/inspection, recordkeeping, reporting, and corrective-action processes that they oversee. It converts the operational principles in the recovered paper-based manual into proposed enterprise rules suitable for controlled digital implementation.

The objectives are to:
1. protect company assets, funds, inventory, and business information;
2. make committee authority and accountability explicit;
3. require evidence-based decisions and approvals;
4. prevent conflicts of interest, unauthorized commitments, fraud, and avoidable error;
5. maintain traceable records from request through final settlement and review;
6. separate transaction execution from independent oversight;
7. ensure findings result in accountable corrective action; and
8. provide a controlled basis for ARNA workflow, permissions, audit trails, and reports.

## 2. Scope and applicability

2.1 This policy applies, once adopted, to Maale Arna S.C. and the relevant ARNA workflows, committees, officers, employees, delegated representatives, and other persons acting for the company within the scope of their authorized responsibilities.

2.2 It governs:
- Board and committee administration to the extent assigned by corporate governing documents;
- procurement planning, supplier sourcing, evaluation, award, ordering, receipt, and payment evidence;
- sales orders, pricing, credit, invoicing, delivery, collection, receivables, and returns;
- control reviews, inspections, evidence handling, findings, corrective actions, and follow-up;
- transaction records, filing, retention, reporting, and auditability; and
- ARNA digital controls supporting these activities.

2.3 It does not create a new legal entity, tenant, committee, office, or corporate power. It does not override applicable law, the company’s articles/bylaws, valid shareholder or Board resolutions, employment rules, financial policies, or a higher-ranking approved policy.

2.4 Where requirements conflict, users must stop the affected action where practicable, preserve the evidence, and escalate to the designated governance/legal authority. No employee or software workflow may resolve a conflict by silently ignoring a higher-ranking requirement.

2.5 This policy applies to manual and digital transactions. Moving a process into ARNA does not remove the requirement for authorization, supporting evidence, or independent review.

## 3. Governing principles

All covered activities must observe these principles:

**3.1 Authority before action.** A person may approve or commit the company only within a valid, recorded delegation and applicable limit.

**3.2 Evidence first.** Decisions, receipts, deliveries, payments, adjustments, and findings must be supported by relevant, authentic, and retrievable evidence.

**3.3 Segregation of duties.** Requesting, approving, sourcing, receiving, recording, paying, and independently reviewing must be separated to the extent practicable. Where staffing makes full separation impossible, a documented compensating review must be approved by an authorized independent person.

**3.4 Traceability.** Every transaction and committee matter must have a unique reference linking its request, decisions, attachments, execution, accounting or inventory records, and review.

**3.5 Integrity of records.** Approved or posted records must not be silently overwritten or backdated. Corrections must preserve the original and identify the reason, actor, date, and required authorization.

**3.6 Independence and impartiality.** Committee members must disclose conflicts and recuse themselves when required. A person must not independently certify work they performed or approve their own transaction.

**3.7 Need-to-know confidentiality.** Access is limited to legitimate duties, with lawful disclosure and escalation exceptions.

**3.8 Proportionality and value.** Decisions must consider business need, total cost, quality, delivery, risk, and authorized budget—not price alone unless the approved procurement method requires it.

**3.9 No unsupported status claims.** A transaction is not “approved,” “received,” “paid,” “reconciled,” “verified,” or “closed” unless the required evidence and authorized action exist.

**3.10 Accountability.** Every decision, exception, finding, and corrective action must have an identifiable responsible person and, where applicable, a deadline.

## 4. Governance hierarchy and authority

4.1 **Board and shareholder authority.** The Board and shareholders retain only the powers assigned to them by applicable law and the company’s governing documents. This policy does not enlarge or transfer those powers.

4.2 **Committee mandate.** Each committee must operate under a written charter or resolution stating its purpose, membership, chair, secretary, quorum, decision method, delegated authority, reporting line, confidentiality duties, term, and limits.

4.3 **Management and operational functions.** Management and designated staff execute approved business activities within their job responsibilities, budget, and recorded delegation.

4.4 **Finance.** Finance performs the financial checks, accounting, payment processing, reconciliation, and reporting assigned by approved finance procedures. Finance verification does not replace the required business approval or independent control.

4.5 **Control Committee.** The Control Committee reviews compliance and evidence independently within its approved mandate. It does not ordinarily initiate, select, execute, receive, pay, or alter the transactions it later examines.

4.6 **Committee secretary.** The secretary maintains notices, agendas, attendance, declared conflicts, minutes, decision registers, evidence references, and follow-up records. The secretary does not gain decision-making authority merely by recording a decision.

4.7 **Delegation.** A delegation must identify the delegator and delegate, authority granted, limits, scope, effective date, expiry/revocation conditions, and approval evidence. Delegation may not be subdelegated unless expressly permitted. Delegation must be reviewed when roles change and must not be treated as permanent merely because a system account has a permission.

4.8 **Reserved and unknown authority.** Monetary thresholds, contract-signing authority, procurement-method thresholds, write-offs, discounts, credit limits, sanctions, and emergency powers remain **TBD pending formal approval**. Until approved, no new threshold may be invented by an operator or encoded as if corporate policy had approved it. Transactions lacking a clear authority path must be escalated and held where lawful and practicable.

## 5. Committee formation and administration

5.1 **Appointment and composition.** Membership, appointment, removal, term, and any independence requirements must be established by the competent authority and recorded in a formal decision. The system must not invent or auto-appoint committee members.

5.2 **Role clarity.** Each committee should identify a chair, members, and secretary, with responsibilities documented in its charter. Substitute or acting appointments must have recorded scope and duration.

5.3 **Quorum and voting.** Quorum, voting rules, casting votes, written resolutions, and treatment of abstentions must be defined in the approved charter or governing documents. If a valid quorum or decision rule cannot be established, the meeting may discuss but must not record a binding decision as approved.

5.4 **Meetings.** Meetings must have an agenda, date/time, attendance, declared conflicts, papers considered, decisions, dissent/abstentions where material, action owners, and deadlines. Minutes must be reviewed and approved according to the charter.

5.5 **Decisions register.** Each decision must have a reference, decision text, authority basis, vote/result where applicable, effective date, owner, conditions, and linked evidence. Draft minutes are not final decisions.

5.6 **Conflict of interest.** Members must disclose actual, potential, or perceived conflicts promptly. The disclosure, recusal, restrictions on access or participation, and decision on the conflict must be recorded. A conflicted member must not influence a decision from which they are required to recuse themselves.

5.7 **Confidentiality.** Committee papers and case evidence must be shared only with authorized recipients. Confidentiality must not be used to suppress lawful reporting of misconduct or material control failures.

5.8 **Escalation.** Suspected fraud, material loss, serious safety/legal risk, evidence tampering, retaliation, or management interference must be promptly escalated through the approved reporting line to an authority independent of the implicated person.

5.9 **Performance review.** The competent authority should periodically review committee attendance, timeliness, quality of decisions, overdue actions, independence, and mandate effectiveness.

## 6. Procurement governance and mandatory workflow

6.1 **Standard workflow.** Procurement must follow the applicable approved method and, at minimum, a traceable sequence:
1. purchase requisition and business justification;
2. budget availability and authority check;
3. specification and procurement-method determination;
4. request for quotation/tender or documented permitted alternative;
5. supplier and quotation register;
6. comparison and evaluation against disclosed criteria;
7. conflict-of-interest declarations;
8. award recommendation and authorized decision;
9. purchase order or contract approval before commitment, except a documented lawful exception;
10. delivery and independent receipt/inspection;
11. invoice and supporting-document verification;
12. authorized payment processing;
13. inventory/asset/accounting update; and
14. filing, reporting, and any required post-procurement review.

6.2 **Requisition.** A requisition must identify the requester and department, date, goods/services, specifications, quantity/unit, required date, estimated cost, purpose, budget reference where applicable, and required approvals.

6.3 **Competition and supplier selection.** The approved procurement procedure must define thresholds and permitted methods. Where quotations are required, the file must preserve each quotation and its date, supplier identity, price, taxes, delivery, quality/technical compliance, warranty, payment terms, and other relevant conditions. The evaluation must use documented criteria and explain the recommendation. The lowest nominal price is not automatically the best value where other approved criteria materially apply.

6.4 **Exceptions and sole source.** Any permitted waiver of competition must state the legal/policy basis, factual justification, value, alternatives considered, conflict checks, approver, and review requirements. No retrospective approval may be used to conceal an unauthorized commitment. Emergency procurement must follow an approved emergency rule and receive the required post-event review.

6.5 **Purchase order and contract.** The PO/contract must link to the requisition and award decision and identify supplier, scope, quantity, price/currency, delivery, payment terms, applicable taxes, and authorized signatures. Changes to scope, price, or duration require documented approval under the applicable authority matrix.

6.6 **Receipt and inspection.** The receiver must compare the delivery against the PO/contract and delivery note, record actual quantity and condition, document shortages/damage/rejections, and identify the receiving and inspecting persons. The person authorizing payment must not rely solely on an unsupported statement that goods were received.

6.7 **Invoice and payment control.** Before payment, the responsible function must perform an appropriate three-way match between PO/contract, receipt/inspection evidence, and invoice, or document the approved alternative for services or other cases where a three-way match is not applicable. Duplicate invoice checks, arithmetic, supplier details, tax documents, approval, and payment evidence must be retained.

6.8 **Supplier integrity.** Supplier selection must be fair, evidence-based, and free of undisclosed personal benefit. Gifts, inducements, collusion, bid manipulation, and use of confidential competitor information are prohibited. Any applicable supplier due diligence must be completed and documented.

6.9 **Procurement file.** The file must contain, as applicable: requisition; budget/authority confirmation; specification; RFQ/tender; all quotations; comparison; evaluation; conflict declarations; award approval; PO/contract and amendments; delivery note; goods received note (GRN); inspection; invoice; payment voucher; proof of payment; inventory/asset entry; exception approvals; and related correspondence.

## 7. Sales, pricing, credit, delivery, and receivables

7.1 **Standard workflow.** A sales file must connect:
1. customer request/order;
2. customer identity and authorized terms;
3. price and discount authorization;
4. credit assessment and approval, if applicable;
5. sales order;
6. invoice/receipt with controlled numbering;
7. delivery note or service-completion evidence;
8. cash collection or receivable entry;
9. customer ledger update and reconciliation; and
10. reporting, returns, refunds, and any follow-up.

7.2 **Customer and order records.** The file must identify the customer, order date, item/service, quantity, unit price, taxes/charges, total, currency, delivery terms, and responsible salesperson. Changes must be traceable.

7.3 **Price and discount controls.** Prices, discount bands, and approvers must be established by an approved schedule or authority matrix. ARNA must not infer approval from a user’s job title or allow users to approve exceptions beyond their delegation. Any exception must include reason, amount/percentage, customer/order reference, approver, and timestamp.

7.4 **Credit sales.** Credit sales require a recorded credit decision under the approved credit policy, including applicable limit, terms, due date, and approver. Over-limit or overdue exceptions must be escalated. Until a credit limit and approval scheme are formally established, the system must not fabricate one; transactions with unclear authority must be held for authorized review.

7.5 **Invoicing and receipts.** Invoices and receipts must have controlled identifiers and link to the sales order. Voided or cancelled documents must remain in the record with the reason and approver; numbers must not be silently reused.

7.6 **Delivery and inventory.** Goods must not be recorded as delivered solely on the basis of an invoice. Delivery or customer acceptance evidence must identify the goods/service, quantity, date, recipient where applicable, and delivery reference. Stock movements must be recorded and reconciled with sales.

7.7 **Collections and receivables.** Customer receipts must be linked to the customer and invoice(s), recorded in the appropriate ledger, and supported by payment evidence. Unapplied receipts, overdue balances, disputes, and credit notes must be visible in reports and assigned for resolution.

7.8 **Returns, refunds, and credit notes.** Returns and refunds must reference the original sale and include reason, evidence of return or service issue, authorization, inventory disposition where applicable, and payment/accounting treatment. Posted sales and receipts must not be erased to make the books appear balanced; use controlled reversal, credit note, or correction entries.

7.9 **Daily and periodic reconciliation.** Sales records, invoices/receipts, collections, cash/bank evidence, stock movement, and customer balances must be reconciled at the frequency set by approved finance procedures. Differences must be recorded, assigned, investigated, and resolved with an audit trail.

## 8. Control Committee mandate and review procedure

8.1 **Purpose and independence.** The Control Committee examines whether activities complied with applicable authority, policy, evidence, and recordkeeping requirements. It is a review and assurance function, not the transaction operator. Its formal powers and reporting line must be specified in its approved charter.

8.2 **Planning.** Each review must have a documented objective, scope, period, criteria, method, sample selection, reviewers, expected evidence, and planned completion date. Risk-based sampling may be used where permitted, but the sample and limitations must be disclosed in the report.

8.3 **Evidence collection.** Evidence must be relevant, attributable, dated where possible, protected against unauthorized alteration, and linked to the case. The reviewer must record missing, contradictory, or inaccessible evidence rather than assuming compliance. Evidence collection must respect applicable law, confidentiality, and access restrictions.

8.4 **Testing.** Reviewers should compare, as relevant:
- requisition, budget, quotations, evaluation, award, PO/contract, receipt, invoice, and payment;
- sales order, authorized price/credit, invoice, delivery, collection, and customer ledger;
- stock records against physical counts and supporting movements;
- approvals against the authority matrix and delegated scope;
- records against bank/cash evidence and accounting entries; and
- previous findings against evidence of corrective action.

8.5 **Finding record.** Each finding must include a unique ID, review reference, criterion, condition observed, supporting evidence, affected transactions/amount where known, cause if established, actual or potential impact, risk rating, responsible function, management response, required corrective action, owner, deadline, and follow-up status. Facts, allegations, analysis, and conclusions must be clearly distinguished.

8.6 **Severity.** The source training manual suggests Critical, Major, and Minor as training classifications. These are not treated as an adopted corporate risk scale until approved. The competent authority must approve definitions, escalation triggers, response deadlines, and reporting requirements. Until then, reviewers may use these labels provisionally and explicitly mark them as provisional.

8.7 **Management response.** The reviewed function must be given a fair opportunity to respond, correct factual errors, and propose actions within a defined timeframe set by the approved procedure. A response does not require the committee to withdraw a supported finding. Unresolved disagreement must be recorded.

8.8 **Reporting.** A report must state the scope and period, methodology/sample, evidence reviewed, limitations, findings, risk and impact, management responses, agreed or disputed actions, owners, due dates, and matters requiring escalation. Reports must be reviewed and issued under the committee charter.

8.9 **Follow-up and closure.** An action may be closed only when the designated independent reviewer verifies adequate evidence that the corrective action was completed and the risk addressed, or the competent authority formally accepts residual risk within its lawful authority. “Management says complete” is not, by itself, closure evidence.

8.10 **Escalation.** Critical or potentially critical matters, suspected fraud, material loss, evidence destruction, retaliation, repeat findings, and overdue high-risk actions must be escalated promptly under an approved escalation matrix. The implicated person must not control the investigation outcome.

8.11 **Limitations.** A control review is limited by its documented scope and evidence. The committee must not claim exhaustive assurance where only samples were tested or relevant evidence was unavailable.

## 9. Conflict of interest, ethics, and anti-retaliation

9.1 All committee members and personnel involved in sourcing, selection, sales, approval, receiving, payment, and review must disclose relevant actual, potential, or perceived conflicts.

9.2 A conflicted person must not participate in evaluation, approval, receipt certification, payment authorization, or review where that participation would compromise impartiality or violate the approved conflict procedure.

9.3 Gifts, bribes, kickbacks, collusion, falsification, concealment of material facts, retaliation against good-faith reporters, and unauthorized use or disclosure of company information are prohibited, subject to applicable law and approved ethics procedures.

9.4 Disclosures and decisions concerning conflicts must be documented and retained with the relevant case or meeting record.

9.5 Allegations must be handled confidentially and fairly. Investigation, employment action, sanctions, and referrals must be conducted only by authorized persons under applicable law and approved procedures. This policy does not itself prescribe or authorize a penalty.

## 10. Records, identifiers, retention, and corrections

10.1 **Unique references.** Each transaction, committee meeting, decision, inspection, finding, corrective action, and report must have a unique, non-ambiguous reference. References must not be reused to conceal cancellation or correction.

10.2 **Suggested reference patterns.** The recovered manual uses:
- `PROC-YYYY-###` — procurement file;
- `SALE-YYYY-###` — sales file;
- `CTRL-YYYY-###` — control/inspection file;
- `RPT-YYYY-MM` — periodic report; and
- `GOV-YYYY-###` — governance meeting/decision file.

These are proposed patterns, not proof that ARNA's identifier service currently implements them. Final patterns must align with ARNA's existing identifier and tenant-scoping standards.

10.3 **File completeness.** Records must contain the required source documents, approvals, attachments, correspondence, exception records, final outputs, and relevant audit references. Missing items must be identified explicitly.

10.4 **Correction.** Paper records must be corrected in a way that preserves the original entry, with date, initials/signature, and reason. Digital records must use controlled correction, reversal, or amendment workflows that preserve the prior value and audit history. Backdating and silent deletion are prohibited.

10.5 **Retention and disposal.** Retention periods must be established by an approved records schedule consistent with applicable law, tax/accounting obligations, contracts, and any legal hold. This draft does not invent a retention period. Records under investigation, dispute, audit, or legal hold must not be destroyed.

10.6 **Access and confidentiality.** Records must be available to authorized reviewers and auditors on a need-to-know basis. Access and disclosure must be logged where supported and required. Confidentiality classifications and external disclosure rules must follow approved corporate policy and law.

10.7 **Archiving.** Physical and digital records must be indexed so a reviewer can retrieve them by reference, date, counterparty, transaction type, or committee case. Backups do not replace required record retention or auditability.

## 11. Reporting and management information

11.1 Reports must have a clear period, source, preparer, reviewer, approval status, and date of generation. Figures must reconcile to authoritative records or disclose the difference and limitation.

11.2 At a minimum, the reporting framework should support:

| Report | Minimum content | Proposed cadence; subject to approval |
|---|---|---|
| Procurement | Requests, orders/contracts, supplier, method, value, status, exceptions, receipts, payments, pending files | Monthly |
| Sales | Cash/credit split, orders, invoices, discounts, returns, collections, receivables, overdue balances | Daily and monthly |
| Inventory | Opening balance, receipts, issues, returns, closing balance, count differences, adjustments | Monthly and after material count events |
| Control Committee | Reviews, sample scope, findings by approved severity, evidence gaps, corrective actions, overdue items | Monthly or quarterly as charter specifies |
| Governance | Meetings, attendance, conflicts, decisions, delegated actions, overdue resolutions | Each meeting and periodic summary |
| Management risk | Material variances, unauthorized exceptions, fraud indicators, unresolved risks, decisions needed | Monthly/quarterly and immediate escalation when material |

11.3 **Report quality.** Reports must distinguish verified facts from estimates, allegations, unresolved differences, and management representations. Provisional results must be labeled provisional. A result must not be labeled “reconciled” unless the required reconciliation was performed and evidenced.

11.4 **Approval and distribution.** Report preparation, review, approval, and distribution responsibilities must be defined. Recipients must be authorized for the data they receive. A report must not be modified after approval without a recorded version/correction trail.

## 12. ARNA digital enforcement requirements

This policy is a governance specification. It does not establish that any control is already implemented in the application. Digital controls must be separately implemented, tested, and evidenced.

12.1 **Identity and tenant boundary.**
- Every action must be attributable to an authenticated identity.
- Records and attachments must be scoped to the correct tenant and permitted organizational context.
- Users must not access another tenant's committee, supplier, customer, transaction, report, or evidence through identifier manipulation or direct API requests.
- Privileged access must be limited, reviewed, and auditable.

12.2 **Role-based authorization.**
- Permissions must distinguish view, create, edit draft, submit, recommend, approve, execute, receive, pay, inspect, report, and close.
- Approval must be checked server-side on every relevant action; hiding a UI button is insufficient.
- Authority must be checked against the transaction type, value, status, tenant, delegation scope, effective dates, and conflict restrictions.
- The platform must deny by default when permission or authority is absent, expired, ambiguous, or invalid.

12.3 **Segregation of duties.**
- The system should prevent a user from approving their own request or transaction where the approved control matrix prohibits it.
- It should prevent the same person from performing incompatible approval, receipt, payment, and independent-review actions, unless a formally approved compensating control applies.
- Any permitted override must capture reason, approver, scope, time, and evidence, and be available for independent review.

12.4 **Workflow and status.**
- Transitions must be explicit and validated server-side.
- A transaction cannot advance without mandatory evidence and approvals.
- A cancelled, rejected, returned, or reversed transaction must retain its history.
- Re-submission after material changes must trigger the required re-approval.
- Posted records must use controlled adjustments/reversals rather than silent mutation.

12.5 **Audit trail.** Record, at minimum where applicable, actor, tenant, action, object/reference, timestamp, prior and new state or a secure change representation, reason, approval reference, and request/correlation identifier. Audit logs must be protected from ordinary users who are subjects of the logs.

12.6 **Evidence handling.** Attachments must be authorized, associated with the correct record, protected from unauthorized replacement, and included in access controls. The system must not represent a missing attachment as evidence received.

12.7 **Reporting and monitoring.** Dashboards must distinguish pending, approved, executed, reconciled, disputed, overdue, and closed states. Material exceptions, repeated failed approvals, overdue actions, and suspicious changes should be surfaced to authorized reviewers.

12.8 **Testing.** Before production release, controls require tests for positive authorization, denied unauthorized access, cross-tenant isolation, self-approval prevention, expired delegation, incomplete evidence, invalid state transitions, audit trail completeness, and correction/reversal integrity. Test evidence must identify the exact tested commit and results.

12.9 **No false implementation claim.** A policy file, UI screen, database column, or passing isolated test is not sufficient evidence that the complete end-to-end control is production-ready. Implementation status must be reported separately as verified, owner-reported, candidate, unknown, or not run.

## 13. Exceptions and emergency handling

13.1 Exceptions are allowed only where an approved policy or applicable law permits them and the decision-maker has authority to approve them.

13.2 Each exception record must state the rule affected, reason, scope, value/impact where relevant, risk assessment, alternatives considered, compensating controls, approver, date/time, expiry or review date, and supporting evidence.

13.3 No exception may waive legal requirements, conceal fraud, authorize a conflict prohibited by law, remove required audit evidence, or grant authority beyond the approver's mandate.

13.4 Emergency actions must be limited to what is necessary, recorded contemporaneously where possible, reported to the appropriate authority, and reviewed afterward under the approved emergency procedure. Retrospective documentation must be labeled as such and must not falsify the original date or sequence.

13.5 If no valid exception authority or procedure exists, the matter must be escalated rather than improvised.

## 14. Non-compliance and corrective action

14.1 Suspected non-compliance must be documented with available evidence and referred to the authorized function or authority for assessment.

14.2 Corrective actions must identify the deficiency, intended outcome, accountable owner, deadline, interim risk control, required evidence, and independent verifier.

14.3 Repeated or overdue findings must be escalated under an approved escalation matrix. Risk acceptance is permitted only by a person with explicit authority, and must state rationale, duration, residual risk, and review date.

14.4 Employment discipline, contract remedies, financial recovery, regulatory notification, and law-enforcement referral must be handled by authorized persons in accordance with applicable law and approved procedures. This policy does not predetermine guilt or establish a sanction schedule.

## 15. Training, communication, and implementation

15.1 Before the policy becomes operational, the competent authority must approve the policy and related charters, authority matrix, procurement procedure, sales/credit procedure, records schedule, risk/severity scale, exception procedure, and escalation rules that it references.

15.2 Relevant members and employees must receive role-specific training. Training should use the source manual's paper forms and scenarios where useful, then map them to the actual ARNA workflow.

15.3 Each team must understand required records, approval limits, conflicts, evidence standards, reporting lines, and how to raise concerns.

15.4 Digital implementation must follow ARNA's controlled change process. Policy approval does not itself authorize deployment, production database changes, or unrelated code changes.

15.5 The implementation owner must maintain a control-to-system mapping that links each approved rule to the relevant permission, workflow, data validation, audit event, report, and test evidence.

## 16. Monitoring, review, and policy change

16.1 The designated policy owner must review this policy at least annually and sooner after a material incident, audit finding, legal change, organizational change, or significant ARNA workflow change.

16.2 Proposed amendments must identify the affected section, reason, risk/impact, dependencies, implementation consequences, and required approval authority.

16.3 No version may be represented as formally approved until the approval record exists. Approved versions must be published in the controlled policy location, with prior versions retained according to the records schedule.

16.4 Changes to a policy must not silently rewrite the rules applicable to an already-approved historical transaction. The applicable policy version and effective date must remain traceable.

## Annex B — Minimum committee meeting record

Every meeting record should include:
- committee name and meeting reference;
- meeting date, time, format, and purpose;
- members and invitees present/absent;
- quorum basis and determination;
- agenda and papers considered;
- conflicts declared and recusals;
- discussion summary sufficient to explain each decision;
- exact decisions, vote/result where applicable, and authority basis;
- dissent or abstention where material and permitted;
- action owner, due date, and evidence required;
- confidentiality/distribution classification;
- preparer, reviewer, approval status, and finalization date.
