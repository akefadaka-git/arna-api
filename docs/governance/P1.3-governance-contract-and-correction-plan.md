## 4. Governance contract and invariants

### G-01 — Canonical natural-person identity
Every appointment and membership references the canonical Person identity, not a parallel/legacy user identity. Identity linkage must remain unique and tenant-safe. Do not create another identity registry as part of governance without an explicit architecture decision.

**Disposition:** REUSE canonical Person relationship; AMEND only where governance code violates the canonical identity contract.

### G-02 — Tenant isolation and authorization
Every read and write is scoped to the validated tenant and relevant governance body. Missing or mismatched tenant context fails closed. A client-supplied tenant/body identifier is not authority. Controllers invoke policy/Gate authorization; service-level checks protect invariants independently of the HTTP route.

**Disposition:** REUSE existing policy foundation; AMEND policy/controller gaps. Regression tests must prove cross-tenant read/write denial, including direct service/API paths.

### G-03 — Governance hierarchy
Bodies can be arranged by approved scope/hierarchy (enterprise, tenant, branch, project or investment where supported). Parent links must not create cycles or cross tenant boundaries. Deactivation must not silently destroy history or orphan active children.

**Disposition:** REUSE body model/service; AMEND validation and service/repository enforcement if current checks are incomplete. Exact allowed hierarchy and parent-child rules are UNRESOLVED until the Owner confirms the policy.

### G-04 — Position identity and uniqueness
Position codes are unique within their governance body. Position identity must be stable and must not be inferred from display names. A position's authority, voting status and officer classification must be validated against the approved model.

**Disposition:** REUSE existing position model/service/repository; AMEND database-level uniqueness or validation only after checking current migration/index definitions. Test duplicate code attempts under concurrent writes.

### G-05 — Term dates and overlap
Terms use explicit effective dates and a documented timezone/date boundary. Current-term lookup must be deterministic. Overlapping effective terms for the same governance body are prohibited by the Owner-approved default. Date inclusivity/timezone semantics and whether a term may exist without a linked active appointment still require source-convention verification or a further policy decision.

**Disposition:** REUSE current term model/repository; AMEND validation/tests to prohibit overlapping effective terms per body. Resolve date inclusivity/timezone from project conventions before implementation. A proposed `GovernanceTermService.php` is not confirmed to exist and must not be assumed; inspect service conventions before deciding whether to CREATE it.

### G-06 — Appointment lifecycle
Appointment transitions follow an explicit state machine. Invalid transitions are rejected; activation/revocation/supersession and their audit events commit atomically. The system must not rely on a read-before-write existence check as the sole protection against concurrent conflicting appointments.

**Disposition:** REUSE current model/service/repository; AMEND transition validation and race-safe enforcement after G-08 decisions. Do not add a redundant `is_active` flag when lifecycle status is authoritative.

### G-07 — Membership lifecycle and overlap
For the same person, position and body, active or suspended memberships with overlapping effective-date ranges are rejected if that is the approved invariant. State transitions must preserve historical records and avoid overlapping active authority. Concurrent requests must be safe across independent database connections.

**Disposition:** REUSE membership model/service/repository; AMEND tests and enforcement where needed. Confirm the intended relationship between membership and appointment before schema expansion.

### G-08 — Primary incumbent and acting authority
The platform must represent the approved rule for one effective primary incumbent per position, with explicitly permitted acting arrangements. Acting appointments need start/end dates, expiry, revocation, scope, and a defined set of delegated powers; they must not accidentally gain all substantive incumbent powers.

**Disposition:** Owner-approved policy. Enforce exactly one effective primary incumbent per position; acting appointments have explicit scope/effective dates/expiry/revocation and only explicitly granted powers. The existing appointment service explicitly does not enforce a global one-active-appointment-per-position rule. AMEND service/repository/model/policy/migration only where evidence requires; use concurrency-safe enforcement and CREATE tests as needed. No implicit shared-primary exception.

### G-09 — Authority matrix and delegation
Authorization must be based on an approved matrix of role/position, scope, action, limits and delegation. Approval workflows must enforce separation of duties, prevent self-approval where required, and fail closed on missing or expired delegation. Monetary limits and currency handling cannot be guessed.

**Disposition:** Owner-approved direction for a scope-based authority matrix with configurable thresholds, separation of duties and auditable delegation. Existing policy documentation says position/membership-derived authority is incomplete. Reuse current policy entry points; inventory existing cross-domain primitives first. Concrete actions, financial thresholds and currency rules remain unresolved; do not hard-code or invent them.

### G-10 — Meetings, quorum and resolutions
Meetings, attendance, quorum, agenda, votes, decisions/resolutions, publication and correction history need explicit lifecycle and authorization contracts. A decision must retain provenance and cannot be silently overwritten. Route documentation identifies meetings as future work.

**Disposition:** DEFER schema and implementation until G-08/G-09 plus quorum, voting and resolution-correction rules are approved. Then CREATE the necessary domain files and tests using existing domain conventions. Do not imply these capabilities already exist.

### G-11 — Audit integrity
Security-relevant changes, approvals, lifecycle transitions and resolution changes need attributable, tenant-scoped audit records. The state change and required audit event must be atomic. Logs must avoid exposing secrets or unnecessary personal data.

**Disposition:** REUSE the established audit infrastructure where verified; AMEND transactional boundaries and tests where required. Exact audit service/table contract remains to be verified before implementation.

### G-12 — Bilingual, accessible client contract
API fields, validation errors, status transitions and pagination must be stable for the Amharic-first, mobile-first client. Public/user-visible labels should support the approved bilingual behavior; authorization and invariant errors must not leak cross-tenant data.

**Disposition:** REUSE app governance screens and API conventions; AMEND API contract and UI integration where mismatches are demonstrated. App build, mobile layout, accessibility and API contract checks remain NOT RUN in this assessment.

## 7. Owner decisions and remaining policy inputs

The four defaults below were approved by the Owner on 2026-10-27. They are binding policy direction for the next implementation-scope proposal, not authorization to change code, schema, production data, merge or deploy.

1. **Primary incumbent — APPROVED:** Exactly one effective primary incumbent per position at a time. No implicit co-chair, shared-primary or vacancy exception. Any exception requires a separate explicit Owner decision before implementation.
2. **Acting authority — APPROVED:** Acting appointments must have explicit scope, effective start/end, expiry and revocation behavior. Acting authority does not inherit all incumbent powers automatically; only explicitly authorized powers apply.
3. **Term overlap — APPROVED:** Overlapping effective terms for the same governance body are prohibited by default. Any exception requires a separately approved, explicit exception model and must be auditable. Inclusive date semantics and timezone details remain to be confirmed against current application/database conventions before implementation; do not invent them.
4. **Authority/delegation — APPROVED:** Use a scope-based authority matrix with configurable thresholds, separation-of-duties controls and auditable delegation. Monetary amounts, currencies and concrete threshold values remain unspecified and must not be invented.

Still requiring policy/architecture decisions before their respective work: historical conflicting-record disposition and data remediation; exact allowed governance hierarchy; term boundary/timezone semantics; concrete authority actions/threshold values/currencies; meeting quorum, voting, publication, correction and retention rules. Meetings/resolutions remain deferred.
