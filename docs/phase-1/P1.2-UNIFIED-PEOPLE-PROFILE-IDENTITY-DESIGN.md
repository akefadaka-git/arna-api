# ARNA OS P1.2 — Unified People Profile and Identity Design Proposal

## 1. Purpose and decision boundary

Define a minimal, auditable target contract for linking a natural person's canonical profile to existing platform accounts, employment records, shareholder records, and governance participation. The purpose is to prevent duplicate identities and accidental privilege inheritance while preserving existing domain ownership and historical records.

This document is design-only. It does **not** authorize:
- schema or migration changes;
- model, service, controller, API, frontend, seeder, or configuration changes;
- data backfills, merges, deletes, or account-linking operations;
- production database access, deployment, PR merge, or gate closure.

Implementation begins only after the Owner approves the design decisions in Section 10 and separately authorizes a bounded implementation unit.

## 2. Current evidence and constraints

Observed in the destination API repository at the baseline above:

- The canonical authentication model is `MaaleArna\\MASES\\Core\\Auth\\Models\\User` at `mases_core/src/Auth/Models/User.php`. PR #10 removed the legacy `App\\Models\\User` class and the `AUTH_MODEL` override.
- The root migration `database/migrations/2026_10_22_000001_create_employees_table.php` defines tenant-scoped employee records, unique `(tenant_id, employee_number)`, and a nullable `user_id` link with `nullOnDelete()`.
- The Shareholder domain has its own registry and service. The shareholder migration has distinct `user_id` and `representative_user_id` semantics; a representative is not the shareholder's direct account link. Shareholder types include natural persons and legal entities.
- Governance already has body, position, term, appointment, and membership structures. `GovernanceMembership` permits a nullable user link and supports non-account identity through member-name/external-reference fields.
- The existing login-identifier resolution path and tenant membership checks must remain the authority for authentication and tenant access. A profile is not an authentication credential.

These are structural observations, not proof that every lifecycle and authorization invariant is already tested. Before implementation, re-read the exact relevant services, constraints, policies, API resources, and tests at the implementation branch SHA.

## 3. Proposed domain contract

### 3.1 Canonical human identity

Introduce a **Person Profile** as the canonical representation of one natural person across ARNA domains. The profile should contain only identity/display/contact attributes approved for this purpose. It must not become a catch-all for domain-specific facts.

Proposed responsibilities:
- provide a stable internal identifier independent of login credentials or domain record numbers;
- support Amharic-first names and optional English equivalents;
- support approved contact details and verification state without making an unverified contact an authentication identifier;
- link to domain records through explicit, auditable relationships;
- preserve identity-link history and prevent silent reassignment.

Do not copy passwords, PINs, MFA secrets, session tokens, tenant grants, financial balances, share quantities, employment status, or governance authority into the Person Profile.

### 3.2 Account and domain records remain distinct

- **User account:** authentication, credentials, account lifecycle, and platform-level identity.
- **Tenant membership / grants:** tenant access and role/permission assignment. Never inferred from a Person Profile link alone.
- **Employee:** employment/HR record, employee number, active status, and employment lifecycle.
- **Shareholder:** legal register record and ownership-domain semantics. A direct self-service user link remains distinct from a representative link.
- **Governance participation:** membership, appointment, term, status, and position history. A person profile link must not grant authority by itself.
- **Legal-entity shareholder:** remains a legal-entity shareholder record; do not misrepresent a company, cooperative, trust, government body, or religious body as a natural-person profile. A separately approved organization/legal-party identity model may be needed later. A natural-person representative can be linked independently.

### 3.3 Relationship shape (logical, not physical schema)

`Person Profile 1 — 0..N domain links`

A profile may be associated with:
- zero or one active User account link, unless the Owner explicitly approves a different cardinality;
- zero or more Employee records over time, with tenant boundaries and historical employment records preserved;
- zero or more governance participation records over time;
- a natural-person Shareholder record where applicable, subject to existing shareholder-register constraints;
- representative relationships to shareholder/legal-entity records without converting the representative into the shareholder.

The physical design (one profile foreign key per domain table versus a typed identity-link table) remains undecided until the Owner approves the cardinality, uniqueness scope, and migration strategy. Avoid a polymorphic link unless its referential-integrity and deletion behavior are explicitly specified and testable.

## 4. Mandatory invariants

1. **No implicit authorization:** linking a profile to a User, Employee, Shareholder, or Governance record grants no login ability, tenant membership, role, permission, ownership, employment status, or decision authority.
2. **Tenant isolation:** every tenant-owned link is scoped and authorized within a validated tenant context. Missing, conflicting, inactive, or unauthorized tenant context fails closed.
3. **Unambiguous identity:** any identifier used for login resolves to at most one eligible account in the required tenant context. Person identity matching must not be used as a substitute for the existing login identifier contract.
4. **Link uniqueness:** one domain record cannot be silently linked to multiple Person Profiles; one account cannot be silently claimed by multiple active profiles if the Owner approves one-profile-per-account cardinality.
5. **Explicit linking:** link and unlink operations are authorized, validated, audited, and protected against cross-tenant references.
6. **Revocation and relinking:** revoking a link invalidates the old association for authorization decisions; relinking requires fresh authorization and must not resurrect stale grants, sessions, cached permissions, or historical authority.
7. **Historical integrity:** unlinking or deactivating a profile must not erase shareholder register history, employment history, governance terms/appointments, or audit records. Physical deletion is not the default.
8. **Representative separation:** a shareholder representative relationship never becomes the shareholder's direct ownership identity or account link.
9. **Lifecycle clarity:** active/inactive/deceased/merged/duplicate states, if required, must have approved semantics. Do not infer that soft deletion means account disablement or that account disablement means profile deletion.
10. **Auditability:** record who performed a link change, when, tenant context, target record types/IDs, action, and reason under the approved audit/privacy policy. Do not log credentials or unnecessary sensitive data.
11. **Concurrency safety:** uniqueness must be enforced at the database layer where possible, with transaction/locking rules for competing link attempts; application validation alone is insufficient.
12. **Localization and accessibility:** Amharic-first labels and errors, mobile-first layouts, keyboard accessibility, and consistent bilingual fallback behavior are required for user-facing changes.

## 5. Proposed lifecycle and service boundaries

A future implementation should route changes through a dedicated application/domain service rather than direct controller model writes. Candidate operations, subject to approval:
- create a profile with validated minimum identity data;
- link an existing account or domain record;
- revoke/unlink an existing association with a reason;
- review identity-link history;
- flag a suspected duplicate for controlled resolution;
- merge duplicate profiles only through a separate, explicitly approved workflow with conflict review and audit trail.

Do not automatically merge records based on matching names, phone numbers, email addresses, or national identifiers. Similarity can generate a review candidate only. Identifier normalization, verification, privacy basis, and allowed matching fields require explicit policy.

The API should expose only fields needed by the caller and enforce server-side authorization per operation. A frontend route or hidden control is not a security boundary.

## 6. Data and migration strategy constraints

Before selecting a physical schema:
1. inventory current User, Employee, Shareholder, GovernanceMembership, representative, and identity-resolution constraints;
2. map all existing identifiers and relationships without changing data;
3. identify natural-person versus legal-entity shareholder cases;
4. decide whether legacy records can be linked deterministically or require manual review;
5. define rollback and forward-fix behavior;
6. confirm the existing flat/root and domain migration discovery conventions so no duplicate migration source is introduced.

No existing row should be automatically assigned to a new profile solely because names or contact fields match. Any backfill must be separately approved, deterministic, dry-run reviewed, idempotent, tenant-safe, and reconcilable. Unknown/conflicting records remain unlinked and are reported for review.

## 9. Risks and unresolved questions

- A single `Person` record is appropriate for natural persons, but shareholder records also represent legal entities. The design must not force legal entities into a human identity abstraction.
- Existing `user_id` and `representative_user_id` relationships encode different legal meanings. A generic identity-link table must preserve those semantics rather than flatten them.
- A profile association is not proof of identity. The accepted verification process and authoritative evidence for linking an existing account remain to be decided.
- One profile per account is a proposed default, not yet an approved rule. Account recovery, duplicate account remediation, and any exceptional multi-account cases need explicit handling.
- The correct tenant scope for a global person profile versus tenant-specific contact/profile fields is unresolved. A global person record can create cross-tenant data exposure if query and response boundaries are not designed carefully.
- The retention/deactivation policy for deceased persons, terminated employees, former shareholders, and ended governance memberships must preserve legal and audit obligations.
- A generic polymorphic link design may weaken referential integrity; a normalized typed relation or explicit foreign keys may be safer. Decide only after examining the current schema and query patterns.

## 10. Owner decisions required before implementation

Please approve, reject, or amend each proposal:

1. **Profile scope:** Person Profile represents natural persons only; legal entities remain in their domain registry unless a separate organization/legal-party profile is approved.
2. **Account cardinality:** at most one active User account is linked to one Person Profile, and one User account is linked to at most one active Person Profile. Exceptions require a separate reviewed workflow.
3. **Tenant model:** profile identity may be globally stable, but tenant-visible fields and every domain link/query must remain tenant-authorized; no cross-tenant profile discovery by default.
4. **Linking authority:** only explicitly authorized service operations may create/revoke links; no name/phone/email-only automatic matching or merge.
5. **History:** link revocation is auditable and does not delete domain history or automatically alter employment/shareholder/governance status.
6. **Implementation order:** read-only file/test inventory first; then schema proposal; then Owner approval; then separately authorized schema/service/API work; then frontend integration.
7. **Duplicate resolution:** candidate detection may be introduced later, but merging profiles is out of scope for the first P1.2 implementation unit.
8. **Audit fields and retention:** confirm that actor, tenant, timestamp, target, action, and reason are required, subject to the platform's approved privacy/retention policy.

Until these decisions are resolved, this document is a proposal, not a final architecture decision record.
